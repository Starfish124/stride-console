# Durabo Discovery Sprint — benchmark draft

August 2026. Toy wholesaler, ~20 staff. Two founders on-site, two days, twelve interview sessions.

## What we measured

One thing, really: repetitive hours. Per person, per week — what repeats, how long it takes, and what they would do with the time back. Not "where could AI help" (everyone guesses at that), but where the hours actually go. The guesses come later, ranked against the hours.

## Method

- 12 interview sessions across 2 days, 45-minute slots, one shared field card for every session.
- Live whisper transcription on our own hardware — the transcript grew during the interview, so follow-up questions came from what was actually said, not from notes.
- Baselines were self-reported first, then clocked where possible. Where we could only get one of the two, the report says so.
- Synthesis: per-person needs from transcripts, every candidate tool scored on a weighted rubric (time saved 30%, cash impact 25%, error reduction 20%, feasibility 15%, future-proof 10%), top 5 kept.
- Deliverable: a 34-page report in Dutch with 9 process flowcharts and the baseline table. The fixed-price proposal is the last chapter of the same document.

## The headline numbers

- **±80–100 hours per week** of repetitive work across ~20 staff. That is roughly **2 to 2.5 FTE** spent on work that repeats identically every week.
- **Sales support:** about **50% of their time** goes to copy-paste work between systems.
- **Quality:** roughly **15 minutes per order** of manual checking, against a **backlog of ~100 orders**.
- **Finance:** **1 to 1.5 days per week** of recurring processing.
- **Design:** an even **50/50 split** between creative work and admin around it.

## What we proposed

Not a transformation program. Five tools, picked because the rubric said so — the weighted score put time saved and cash impact in front, feasibility kept the moonshots honest, and future-proofing broke ties. Each of the five traces back to named hours in the baseline table, so the client can check our math against their own week.

## Honest limits

- **Baselines are largely self-reported.** People round up when they are frustrated and round down when they are embarrassed. We clocked what we could and marked the rest, but ±80–100 is a range for a reason.
- **Whisper transcripts were a working feed, not a record.** Live transcription is good enough to steer an interview and pull verbatim quotes from; it is not a certified minute. Group moments in the room degrade it — which is why we logged who was present in every session.
- Two days is a sample, not a census. A week with an unusual order load looks different from a quiet one.

## What this proves about the method

You can walk into a ~20-person company you have never seen, and in two days leave with a per-person map of where the repetitive hours are, evidence in the people's own words, and a ranked shortlist the owner can argue with — because every rank has a score and every score has a source. The proposal writes itself at that point, which is exactly the idea: the report is the proposal. That is repeatable at any company this size, in their language, at a fixed price. The only thing that changes per client is the roster, the rubric weights, and the language of the report.
